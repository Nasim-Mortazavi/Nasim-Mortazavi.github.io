About

I am a Ph.D. Candidate in Neuroscience at Western University (Schulich School of Medicine & Dentistry), working with stereo-EEG recordings from patients with temporal lobe epilepsy. My thesis investigates how hippocampal CA1 sharp-wave ripples support associative encoding and memory consolidation during virtual spatial navigation tasks.

My background spans theoretical particle physics, computational physics, and clinical neuroscience — a combination that shapes a rigorous, model-first approach to every analysis. I build end-to-end pipelines from raw clinical data through statistical modelling and communicate results across neuroscience, clinical, and engineering teams.

Canadian Permanent Resident, based in London, Ontario. Available without sponsorship. Fluent in English; native Farsi speaker.

Research

Hippocampal Memory Dynamics

Characterising how CA1 sharp-wave ripples and theta oscillations support associative encoding and recall during SEEG-implanted patient studies.

SEEG / iEEG Sharp-Wave Ripples CA1 Associative Memory

Interictal Discharge Detection & Impact

Algorithms to separate physiological ripples from pathological IEDs, and quantifying how IED burden disrupts hippocampal encoding signals and memory performance.

IED Detection SWR Classification PyTorch CNN Epilepsy

Statistical Modelling of Neural Events

GLMMs, Bayesian inference, and state-space models linking trial-level neural features to behaviour — accounting for subject variability, electrode nesting, and learning trajectories.

GLMM / lme4 Bayesian Inference State-Space Models

Human Single-Neuron Recording Programmes

Building the clinical infrastructure and spike-sorting pipelines for single-unit activity from hybrid macro/micro-electrodes — among Canada's first such programmes.

Single-Unit Recording Spike Sorting Clinical Infrastructure
