About

Physics-trained, neuroscience-driven, and computationally focused.

I am a computational scientist trained across physics, neuroscience, and data-driven modeling. My work is guided by a long-standing fascination with how complex systems work — from physical systems to the human brain — and by the belief that rigorous science and modern AI can be used to improve human life.

My current research studies human associative memory using intracranial SEEG recordings from the hippocampus, with a focus on sharp-wave ripples, spatial learning, and statistical modeling of neural and behavioral data.

Beyond academic neuroscience, I build computational tools for health-related data: reproducible analysis pipelines, mixed-effects models, machine-learning workflows, neural-signal classifiers, and interactive visualization tools. I am interested in AI not as a trend, but as a scientific tool — one that can help make complex biological and clinical data more useful, interpretable, and actionable for human health.

Academic Research

Human hippocampal dynamics, associative memory, and learning circuitry.

How does the human hippocampus organize learning?

My academic work asks how hippocampal sharp-wave ripples reorganize during associative spatial learning, and how this activity relates to behavioral performance, anatomical location, and memory formation.

The central focus is human CA1: a hippocampal subfield where intracranial recordings allow direct measurement of fast neural events during learning.

Sharp-wave ripples and associative spatial learning

Human CA1 · SEEG · Hippocampal dynamics

How ripple activity in human CA1 reorganizes during learning: post-learning ripple-rate ramping, posterior predominance, and the link between ripple rate and behavioral performance.

Localizing electrodes within hippocampal subfields

HippUnfold · CT–MRI coregistration · Anatomical precision

A quantitative method for placing intracranial contacts within hippocampal subfields, resolving CA1 channel assignment across subjects.

Anterior–posterior functional differentiation

Long-axis organization · Spatial learning · Ripple dynamics

How position along the hippocampal long axis shapes ripple dynamics and the encoding of spatial zones.

Projects

These projects reflect the applied side of my work: building tools that transform complex health, behavioral, and electrophysiological data into interpretable models, visualizations, and reproducible workflows.

GLMMs for health and behavioral data

A statistical modeling project focused on generalized linear mixed-effects models for repeated-measures health and behavioral datasets. The emphasis is reproducible analysis, subject-level variability, interpretable model outputs, and publication-quality visualization.

1D-CNN for ripple classification

A Python-based deep learning pipeline for classifying hippocampal ripple events from one-dimensional electrophysiological signals. The project includes signal preprocessing, model training, evaluation workflows, and comparison with rule-based ripple detection.
